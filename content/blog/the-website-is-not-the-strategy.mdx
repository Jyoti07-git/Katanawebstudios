A website can carry an extraordinary amount of expectation. It has to tell a story, build trust, answer questions, create momentum, and look memorable while it does it.

That is precisely why beginning with a homepage reference is so often a dead end. A reference can help define taste, but it cannot define the job.

## Start with the change

Before a single component is drawn, we ask one useful question: **what needs to be different after a visitor spends time here?**

Sometimes the answer is practical: more qualified project enquiries, better conversion from a campaign, fewer repetitive sales conversations. Other times it is perceptual: a growing business needs to look as capable as it has become.

Either way, that desired change gives the work a centre of gravity. It tells us what to lead with, what can wait, and what the next click should mean.

## Design is the evidence

Visual choices are not decoration on top of strategy. They are how strategy becomes felt. An editorial layout can communicate calm expertise. A direct headline can make a complex offer suddenly legible. A small motion detail can make a brand seem unexpectedly alive.

The important part is that every choice is pointing in the same direction. When the copy says “precise” and the interface feels improvised, visitors notice—even if they cannot put a name to the mismatch.

## Give the site something to do

At Katana, we treat a site as a working part of the business. It has an audience, a point of view, and a next action. That means every project begins with intent and ends with a digital experience that can prove it.

The pixels are the result. The strategy is the reason they work.
